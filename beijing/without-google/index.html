Getting around Beijing without Google

Last checked: 6 November 2026

Does Google work in Beijing?

No. Google Maps, Gmail, Search and YouTube are blocked in mainland China, and a VPN on hotel or local Wi-Fi is unreliable. The fixes are simple if you set them up before you fly: a mobile data plan that works in China, Apple Maps or Amap for directions, DiDi for taxis, and Alipay or WeChat Pay for paying. Roaming data and many travel eSIMs route through outside China, so some blocked apps work on them, but do not count on it.

Which map app should I use?

  • Apple Maps works well in Beijing on iPhone, in English, with metro and walking directions. It uses Chinese map data, so locations line up correctly.
  • Amap (高德地图) is the most detailed, and is good for buses and walking routes.
  • Google Maps, even with a connection, shows streets offset by a few hundred metres because of differences in coordinate systems. Do not navigate with it.

Save your hotel's address in Chinese before you go; English names are often not understood by drivers.

How do I take a taxi?

DiDi is China's ride-hailing app, with an English version, and also available inside Alipay, and accepts linked foreign cards. Street taxis are plentiful and cheap; drivers rarely speak English, so show your destination in Chinese. Penyola keeps a Chinese address card for each sight it covers, and our address cards page has the Forbidden City, Jingshan Park and Summer Palace ready to show.

How do I pay?

Most places in Beijing expect mobile payment. Alipay and WeChat Pay both let visitors link a foreign Visa or Mastercard after a passport check; set them up and make a small test payment before your trip. Carry some cash as a backup: businesses are required to accept it, though small stalls may have no change.

How do I use the metro?

The metro is fast, cheap and signed in English. Pay with the Beijing transit QR code inside Alipay or buy a single ticket from a machine. Every station has a security check of bags.

What about translation?

Apple Translate works offline once you download Chinese; Google Translate needs a connection that reaches Google. Baidu Translate and the translation inside WeChat work on local networks. Photo translation of menus and signs is the feature you will use most.

What should I download before I go?

  • Alipay and/or WeChat, with a card linked and a test payment made.
  • DiDi, or use it inside Alipay.
  • Amap, as a backup to Apple Maps.
  • An offline translator with Chinese downloaded.
  • Offline guides for the sights you plan to see, so a weak signal does not matter.
