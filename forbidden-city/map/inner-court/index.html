North of the great halls, the inner court was where the emperor and empress lived. All five spots are on the Classic route, one after another up the middle, ending in the Imperial Garden just before the north exit.

7. Gate of Heavenly Purity

The gate into the inner palace. A pair of gilded bronze lions marks the change; compare their bright surfaces with the darker bronze lions you passed further south.

8. Palace of Heavenly Purity

Built as the emperor's residence. After the Yongzheng Emperor moved to the Hall of Mental Cultivation, it was used for audiences, government business and banquets. Above the throne hangs a plaque reading Zhengda Guangming, "upright and bright"; under the Qing system of secret succession, the sealed name of the chosen heir was kept behind it.

9. Hall of Union

The small square hall between the emperor's palace and the empress's. Qing empresses received formal greetings here, and the court kept its twenty-five imperial seals here, in cases on either side of the throne. It also holds two ways of telling time: a copper water clock and a large striking clock.

10. Palace of Earthly Tranquility

The empress's residence in the Ming dynasty. After the Qing rebuilt it, most of the rooms were used for Manchu shamanic rites, while the eastern rooms kept an imperial wedding chamber.

11. Imperial Garden

Laid out in 1420, at the north end of the central axis, with Qin'an Hall in the middle. Look down at the paths, made of pebble pictures in different colours, and find the two cypresses whose trunks have grown together. The Gate of Divine Might, the north exit, is just beyond.

Tickets and opening

Everyone enters at the Meridian Gate and leaves by the Gate of Divine Might or the East Glorious Gate. The palace is closed on Mondays, except public holidays. Book your entry ticket online with your passport; see how to book.
