Forbidden City map: the outer court

Last checked: 11 November 2026

The outer court is the southern half of the palace, where the court held its great ceremonies. You walk into it straight after the Meridian Gate. Spots 1 to 4 are on the Classic route up the middle; 5 and 6 sit off to the west and east, behind their own gates.

1. Bronze lions

A pair of Ming dynasty bronze lions guards the approach to the Gate of Supreme Harmony. Look under their paws: one rests a paw on a ball, the other has a cub. The gate behind them has been closed for building work since 3 August 2026, but you see the lions from the square in front.

2. Hall of Supreme Harmony

The biggest hall in the palace, used for enthronements, imperial weddings and the other great ceremonies. It burned and was rebuilt more than once; what stands today follows the rebuilding of 1695. Count the ten little beasts on the roof corners, then look along the terrace for the sundial, the standard measure and the bronze tortoises and cranes. More in the Hall of Supreme Harmony and the roof guardians.

3. Hall of Central Harmony

The small square hall between the two big ones. Before a grand ceremony the emperor stopped here to rest, go over the procedures and get ready. Its small size is the point: a pause between two very large buildings.

4. Hall of Preserving Harmony

Court banquets were held here, and from the Qianlong reign the final round of the highest imperial examination. Walk round the back for the largest carved stone ramp in the palace, dragons moving through clouds down a single sloping slab. You see it from the path; it is part of the stairway, not a sculpture you can walk round.

5. Hall of Military Prowess

A palace complex west of the great halls. It became the centre of imperial book production, and the movable-type editions printed here were known as Wuying Hall editions. The Palace Museum closed it from 19 October 2026 for repairs and conservation.

6. Hall of Literary Glory

On the east side, near the East Glorious Gate exit. In the Ming and Qing dynasties the court held spring and autumn lectures here: officials explained passages from the Confucian classics and the emperor replied.

Tickets and opening

Everyone enters at the Meridian Gate and leaves by the Gate of Divine Might or the East Glorious Gate. The palace is closed on Mondays, except public holidays. Book your entry ticket online with your passport; see how to book.
